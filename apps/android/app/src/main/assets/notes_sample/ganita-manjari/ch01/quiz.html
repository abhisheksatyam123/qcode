Chapter 1 — Orienting Yourself: The Use of Coordinates

A coordinate system lets us turn places into numbers. This chapter builds the 2-D Cartesian plane, quadrants, ordered pairs (x, y), and distance via Baudhāyana–Pythagoras.

Concept map

WhyLocate a point in a plane with two numbers, not vague words.
Axesx-axis (horizontal) and y-axis (vertical) meet at the origin O(0, 0).
QuadrantsI (+,+), II (−,+), III (−,−), IV (+,−).
Distance√[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]

Remember

  • x-coordinate = perpendicular distance from the y-axis (signed).
  • y-coordinate = perpendicular distance from the x-axis (signed).
  • On the y-axis, x = 0; on the x-axis, y = 0.
  • Example from the text: A(3,4) to D(7,1) → Δx=4, Δy=−3 → distance = √(16+9) = 5.

d = √( (x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)² )
